The problem

Paying people in Kenya still costs too much and takes too long.

European companies rely on talented developers, designers, support teams and suppliers in Kenya. Paying them usually means an international bank transfer: fees on both ends, an exchange rate you only see afterwards, and money that can take days to arrive.

8.78%

14.55%

Average cost when the money is sent through a bank, the most expensive type of provider.

Source: World Bank, Remittance Prices Worldwide, Q1 2025. Figures are for sending the equivalent of US$200.

How it works

Three steps, and you always know what they'll receive.

  1. Add who you're paying

    Their name and either an M-Pesa number or Kenyan bank details. Save them once and pay them again in seconds.

  2. See the rate and fee first

    Before you confirm, you see the exchange rate, our fee and exactly how many shillings will arrive. No surprises afterwards.

  3. Pay in euros. They get shillings.

    Fund the payment from your business account. The money lands in their M-Pesa wallet or bank account, and our aim is within the hour.

Questions

What you might be wondering.

Something else on your mind? Email us and you'll hear back from the founder.

Is Four Pillars Digital live yet?

Not yet. We're preparing a pilot with a small group of European businesses in early 2027. Join the pilot list and we'll be in touch as soon as places open.

Who will hold my money?

Customer money will be handled by licensed and regulated payment partners, not by Four Pillars Digital directly. We'll name our partners here once agreements are in place.

What will it cost?

You'll see the exact exchange rate and our fee before every payment. Pilot pricing will be shared with pilot customers, and our aim is to be clearly cheaper than a bank transfer.

Which countries do you cover?

To start, businesses in Ireland and the eurozone paying people and companies in Kenya. Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda are next.

How do recipients get paid?

In Kenyan shillings, straight to an M-Pesa wallet or a Kenyan bank account. They don't need a foreign account or any new app.

Do I need to use crypto?

No. You pay in euros and recipients receive shillings. Behind the scenes we may use regulated digital settlement to make payments faster, but you never need to hold or handle crypto.
